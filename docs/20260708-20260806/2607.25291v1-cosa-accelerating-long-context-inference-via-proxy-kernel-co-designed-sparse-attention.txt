Abstract 

The quadratic cost of self-attention makes long-context infer-ence prohibitively expensive, and proxy-based block-sparse attention has become a practical remedy. Existing methods typically rely on a proxy to predict a binary sparse mask and a kernel to consume this mask and perform sparse attention computation. Such an approach is effective under moderate budgets. However, as the budget tightens, the estimated proxy inevitably drops some salient blocks, while the kernel can only apply the sparse mask mechanically, leading to an evi-dent drop in model accuracy. We propose CoSA, a two-stage training-free Sparse Attention under proxy-kernel CO-design, which couples a Kernel-Aware Proxy (KAP) with an Ordered-Skipping Kernel (OSK). In the first stage, the KAP selects blocks under a moderate budget and produces an ordered mask that prescribes the order in which KV pages are visited in the kernel inner loop. In the second stage, the OSK applies this mask and skips more blocks under a tightened budget given online-softmax statistics. Across mainstream LLM backbones and long-context benchmarks, CoSA attains higher accuracy at lower budgets. Impressively, CoSA achieves a 4.93 × atten-tion speedup and reduces end-to-end Time-to-First-Token by 2.53 × under a context length of 128K with negligible perfor-mance degradation. 

1 Introduction 

Long context capabilities become the foundation for mod-ern Large Language Models (LLMs), which are increasingly demanded by advanced use cases like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) (Zheng et al. 2025; Du et al. 2026) and autonomous agentic systems (Team et al. 2025; Zeng et al. 2026). However, the standard self-attention inherently incurs a computation cost that increases quadratically with respect to the sequence length, leading to unbearable inference la-tency for long inputs. Sparse attention exploits the inherent sparsity of attention maps by selectively computing only the important query-key block interactions. Existing methods generally fall into training-free and trainable approaches. Trainable sparse at-tention learns which blocks to attend to via distillation (Gao et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2026; Tang et al. 2026) or directly introduces sparse computation to pretraining. However, the training burden becomes problematic given the diverse array of model backbones, making training-based sparsification costly to deploy.

The prevailing strategy uses a cheap proxy to estimate attention importance online, and it produces a bi-nary sparse mask according to the sparsity budget. The sparse attention kernel then follows this mask to perform the sparse attention computation (Jiang et al. 2024; Lai et al. 2025; Fan et al. 2026; Xu et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025). Such proxies are reliable under moderate budgets. However, as the bud-get tightens, they increasingly miss the truly salient blocks. There also exists a sparse strategy that skips blocks inside the kernel using the exact Softmax statistics (Yuan et al. 2025b; Zhang et al. 2025a). Though the skipping strategy is oracle-aligned, it still needs to compute the full query-key interactions. Moreover, it applies a conservative skip rule, which leaves much of the potential speedup on the table. To reconcile this efficiency-fidelity trade-off, we propose CoSA, a two-stage training-free block-sparse attention for long-context inference built on the principle of proxy-kernel co-design . Our design starts from inherent properties of block-sparse attention and limitations of in-kernel skipping (Sec. 3), which together argue for designing the proxy and kernel jointly rather than in isolation. The bridge between them is a single computation-order mask that replaces the conventional binary block mask. Specifically, CoSA couples 

Kernel-Aware Proxy (KAP) and Ordered-Skipping Kernel (OSK) . The KAP prunes redundant dense query-key (QK) interactions under a moderate budget, which we call the first-stage sparsity. Besides marking which blocks to com-arXiv:2607.25291v1 [cs.CL] 28 Jul 2026 pute, it also prescribes the order in which the selected blocks are visited in the block-sparse kernel loop. The OSK then uses this computation-order mask via lightweight page-table remapping. On top of the computation-order mask, the OSK uses the exact in-kernel logits to skip even more blocks. This applies a second-stage sparsity that pushes the sparse budget lower. Overall, the proxy shapes the kernel by dic-tating its execution order, while the kernel shapes the proxy by consuming its mask to drive sparsity. This mutual shap-ing illustrates the principle of proxy-kernel co-design. Our contributions are as follows: 

• We revisit inherent properties of block-sparse attention and limitations of in-kernel skipping, revealing that the disconnection between the proxy and backend is the fun-damental bottleneck to increasing sparsity while main-taining high accuracy. 

• We design KAP, a proxy that emits a computation-order mask. It selects sparse blocks under a moderate budget, which realizes the first-stage sparse selection, and pre-scribes their visiting orders in the kernel. 

• We design OSK, an optimized kernel that echoes KAP by physically jumping over any-order pages based on the computation-order mask. It performs an in-kernel skip on softmax statistics, which carries out the second-stage sparse computation. 

• We conduct extensive experiments on mainstream LLM backbones and benchmarks, demonstrating superior ac-curacy at lower budgets. For example, CoSA delivers a 4.93 × attention speedup and 2.53 × end-to-end prefilling speedup at a 128K context length with negligible perfor-mance degradation. 

2 Related Works 

2.1 Sparse Attention 

Sparse attention skips unimportant query-key block interac-tions, and existing methods differ mainly in how the retained blocks are identified. MInference (Jiang et al. 2024) estimates the optimal block indices at inference time from three pre-defined patterns, and FlexPrefill (Lai et al. 2025) extends it to a query-aware variant. XAttention (Xu et al. 2025) refines the pooling-based proxy with finer-grained antidiagonal scoring. Other works instead explore dynamic budget allocation (Niu et al. 2026), token-level sparsity (Liu et al. 2026a), or head heterogeneity (Wang et al. 2025; Liu, Cao, and He 2025; Liu et al. 2026b). Effective as they are, all of these methods are inserted before a standard sparse-attention kernel and leave the kernel design itself untouched. 

2.2 High-Performance Attention Backend 

Beyond algorithm-level optimization, a parallel line of works optimizes the attention kernel itself. The FlashAttention se-ries (Dao et al. 2022; Dao 2024; Shah et al. 2024; Zadouri et al. 2026) tile the computation and fuse the online-softmax (OSM) to cut traffic to GPU global memory, delivering large speedups without altering the result. 4 CoSA 

4.1 Overview 

Guided by the properties and limitations of Sec. 3, we pro-pose CoSA, a two-stage sparse attention built on proxy-kernel co-design. On the proxy side , to counteract L2 , KAP lever-ages order-invariance ( P2 ) to outputs an ordered mask MKAP 

placing “have rowmax” (HRM) blocks first. Blocks filtered by MKAP with moderate stage-1 budget BStage-1 are directly skipped. On the kernel side , OSK applies this mask and further skips value-side computation following Eq. (5), de-noted as stage-2 sparse computation. As shown in Figure 4, this yields three block types in OSK: ➀ mask-skipping, ➁

in-kernel skipping, and ➂ fully-computed. Algorithm A in Appendix C summarizes the workflow.

4.3 Ordered-Skipping Kernel 

The OSK is the kernel-side half of our co-design, shaped by 

MKAP so that the proxy’s decisions become physical execu-tion. It features three computation patterns: ➀ mask-skipped, 

➁ in-kernel skipped, and ➂ computed, as shown in Figure 4. We implement the OSK with the following designs. 

Physical jumping over pages. Given MKAP under a mod-erate budget ( P1 ), the OSK partly skips the costly QK multiplication that in-kernel skipping alone can never save (L1 ). Recall that the Block-Sparse-Attention kernel (Guo et al. 2024) traverses every block and drops the unselected ones through a logical mask, so its inner loop keeps paying the control-flow and synchronization cost even on skipped blocks. The OSK instead jumps directly to the next retained page and thereby removes these redundant synchronizations. Our jump is a page remap over the paged KV cache, and its visiting order is dictated by MKAP .

In-kernel logic skip. On top of the MKAP -driven skip-ping, the OSK further supports in-kernel skipping of Eq. (5) to the blocks kept by stage-1 filtering. It realizes the stage-2 sparsification from the real online-softmax statistics rather than a proxy estimate. We implement this threshold-based skip following (Yuan et al. 2025b; Zhang et al. 2025a), and further tailor its granularity to each 128-row query tile. Two consumer warp groups evaluate 64 query rows each and com-bine their partial predicates into a single tile-wide vote with negligible decision overhead. 

Any-order page visiting. To counteract the conservative skipping rule ( L2 ), the OSK visits pages in the order pre-scribed by KAP, which places the HRM blocks first so that the running maximum rises early and the subsequent in-kernel skip becomes both safer and more aggressive. Reordering the value accumulation leaves the output unchanged, as guaran-teed by the order-invariance of the OSM ( P2 ), so this re-ordering is correctness-preserving. 5 Experiments 

5.1 Setup 

Baselines and models. We compare CoSA against strong block-sparse attention baselines, MInference (Jiang et al. 2024), FlexPrefill (Lai et al. 2025), and XAttention (Xu et al. 2025), with FlashAttention-2 (Dao 2024) as the full-attention baseline. We apply all methods to two representative back-bones, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al. 2024) and Qwen3-8B (Yang et al. 2025). For Qwen, we adopt the recom-mended sampling parameters and enable the thinking mode as each task requires, extending the context to 128K tokens with YaRN (Peng et al. 2023) where necessary. 

Benchmarks. We evaluate CoSA on widely recog-nized synthetic and real-world long-context benchmarks. RULER (Hsieh et al. 2024) provides synthetic tasks with token lengths ranging from 4K to 128K, while LongBench-v2 targets real-world deep understanding and reasoning. On LongBench-v2, we report both the direct ( w/o Chain-of-Thought, CoT) and the thinking ( w/ CoT) modes, probing the reasoning robustness of sparse prefilling. 

Implementation details. Because the quadratic attention cost is concentrated in prefilling, our evaluation scope, in line with prior sparse-prefill studies (Jiang et al. 2024; Lai et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2025), sparsifies only the prefilling stage and leaves decoding dense. We implement the KAP scoring and pooling operations as a fused kernel to minimize proxy overhead. The OSK operates directly on the paged KV cache and supports page visits in the order prescribed by KAP. All accuracy and speed measurements are taken on an NVIDIA H20 node. 5.2 Accuracy Results 

Ruler. Table 1 reports RULER accuracy from 4K to 128K on Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B. CoSA achieves the strongest average among the compared sparse baselines while using the lowest budget. More revealing than the averages is how this gap evolves with context length. At short contexts, every method sits close to full attention, so sparsity comes al-most for free. However, as the sequence length increases, the baselines discard increasingly more salient blocks, resulting in substantial accuracy degradation. B Related Works 

B.1 Training-Free Sparse Attention 

Training-free sparse prefilling relies on a lightweight proxy that predicts block importance before the attention kernel runs. MInference (Jiang et al. 2024) and FlexPrefill (Lai et al. 2025) locate salient regions through vertical-slash (VS) and block-sparse pattern identification, while XAtten-tion (Xu et al. 2025) captures both patterns by scoring each block along its antidiagonal. Several works instead refine the proxy itself: Stem (Niu et al. 2026) augments it with value-aware selection and position-aware budget allocation, ProxyAttn (Wang et al. 2025) reduces its cost along the head dimension, and FlashPrefill (Fan et al. 2026) thresholds block scores to accommodate the long-tailed distribution of post-softmax logits. Beyond block-wise sparsity, VecAtten-tion (Liu et al. 2026a) explores token-level sparsity to accel-erate multimodal prefilling. A complementary line further extends sparsity to the decoding stage under long reasoning contexts (Tang et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2025; Luo et al. 2025). 

B.2 Distillation-based Sparse Attention 

Distillation-based sparsification keeps the backbone frozen and trains auxiliary modules that allocate sparsity dynam-ically. DuoAttention (Xiao et al. 2024) and SwiAttn (Zhao et al. 2026) learn layer-wise routers that switch between dense attention and sliding-window attention (SWA), whereas Elas-tic Attention (Tang et al. 2026) trains a task-aware router that grants a larger budget to harder tasks. RTTurbo (Zhou et al. 2026) exploits head-wise heterogeneity in sparsity to acceler-ate both prefilling and decoding, and SSA (Shen et al. 2025) aligns intermediate hidden features rather than performing end-to-end distillation for more efficient adaptation. 

B.3 Trainable Sparse Attention 

Beyond post-training adaptation, another line builds sparsity directly into the model during pretraining. NSA (Yuan et al. 2025a) and MoBA (Lu et al. 2026) treat KV blocks as ex-perts and activate only the salient ones at both the training and inference stages. DeepSeek-V3.2 refines NSA into finer-grained token-level selection, termed DeepSeek Sparse At-tention (DSA), and DeepSeek-V4 further compresses atten-tion through a hybrid of Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA) and Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA). HySparse (Gao et al. 2026) interleaves dense and sparse layers and reuses the KV states of the dense layers, while MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA) realizes trainable block-sparse attention on top of grouped-query attention (GQA).

B.4 High-Performance Attention Backend 

Beyond algorithm-level optimization, a parallel line of work optimizes the attention kernel itself. The FlashAttention se-ries (Dao et al. 2022; Dao 2024; Shah et al. 2024; Zadouri et al. 2026) tiles the computation and fuses the online soft-max to cut traffic to GPU global memory, delivering large speedups without altering the result. SageAttention-2 (Zhang et al. 2024) accelerates attention through nearly lossless INT4 quantization with outlier smoothing, while RingAt-tention (Liu, Zaharia, and Abbeel 2023) distributes long sequences across multiple devices and overlaps the com-munication. MIT Block-Sparse-Attention (Guo et al. 2024) supports streaming and arbitrary block masks for efficient prefilling. FlashInfer (Ye et al. 2025) offers a customizable engine with block-sparse and paged KV-cache formats, and PagedAttention (Kwon et al. 2023) stores the KV cache in non-contiguous pages to remove fragmentation during serv-ing. BLASST (Yuan et al. 2025b) reuses the online-softmax statistics to skip negligible blocks inside the kernel with near-zero decision overhead on modern GPUs. Table A summarizes how representative backends realize sparse attention. MIT Block-Sparse-Attention performs logi-cal mask skipping, whereas FlashPrefill and SpargeAttention physically jump to retained blocks. BLASST and SpargeAt-tention additionally support in-kernel skipping. Among the compared backends, OSK is the only one that combines phys-ical page jumping, in-kernel skipping (IKS) based on exact logits, and any-order page visiting (P-RMP). This combina-tion allows OSK to consume KAP’s computation-order mask rather than a conventional binary mask.

D Experimental Details and Additional Results 

D.1 Experimental Configurations 

CoSA settings. We use logical query, key, and value blocks of b = 128 tokens. The physical KV-cache page size bp is configured independently by the paged-KV layout; a logical key/value block is resolved through one or more physical pages when bp differs from b. Our SM90 kernel adopts a warp-specialized organization. Two consumer warp groups jointly process one 128-row query tile, with each consumer warp group responsible for 64 query rows. Each consumer warp group first reduces its local row-wise skip predicates, after which OSK combines the two partial results into a sin-gle tile-wide skip decision. This realizes the per-128 skip semantics of Eq. (5). The reduction directly reuses the on-chip row-wise maxima maintained by online softmax and in-troduces no additional score computation or global-memory traffic. For Qwen3-8B, Stage-1 selection uses Top-p with p =0.95 , and the main operating point uses ∆ = 2000 in the length-adaptive threshold ∆/N . For Llama-3.1-8B, the Stage-1 p values are calibrated offline by context length fol-lowing (Xu et al. 2025), and we set ∆ = 200 . Setting ∆ = −1

disables in-kernel skipping and serves as the Stage-1-only endpoint in the remapping analysis.
