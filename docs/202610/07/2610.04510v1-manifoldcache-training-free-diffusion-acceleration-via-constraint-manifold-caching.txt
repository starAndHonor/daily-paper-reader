# Abstract 

Diffusion models for structured scientific generation must produce samples satis-fying hard geometric constraints imposed by physics, chemistry, or biology, yet inference in these settings is prohibitively slow, demanding hundreds to thousands of neural-function evaluations per sample. We unify eight state-of-the-art models spanning medical volumetrics, molecular conformations, protein backbone design, crystal structure prediction, and multi-view 3D scenes under a single abstraction, 

Constraint-Manifold Diffusion Models (CMDMs), in which the target distribution is supported on a manifold defined by an externally specified constraint map. Existing acceleration families transfer poorly to this class: quantization exhausts memory on high-dimensional volumetric operators; pruning breaks constraint fidelity; fast ODE solvers allow trajectories to drift off the constraint manifold; and feature-caching heuristics are blind to constraint geometry and can cause mode confusion in the high-noise regime. We introduce M ANIFOLD CACHE , the first training-free, data-free accelerator designed from first principles for CMDMs. The key insight is that the conditional score decomposes orthogonally into a normal component, which enforces constraint satisfaction, and a tangential component, which navigates within the manifold. Building on this structure, we show for a two-mode Gaussian model that the noise-schedule midpoint, where signal and noise have equal energy, separates two regimes: once signal dominates, the error of a cached estimate is provably bounded, while before that point a strictly positive fraction of cached estimates land in the wrong mode, and this fraction does not vanish as the midpoint is approached. We further prove that, under an explicit condition on the balance between the two score components, deeper network blocks admit larger certified cache strides, as a consequence of the score decomposition propagating through block Jacobians. The resulting schedule requires one integer comparison per block per step, zero calibration data, and zero training overhead. Across all eight CMDMs, MANIFOLD CACHE delivers wall-clock speedups of 1.2–1.9× and stays closest to full-inference quality on each model’s primary metric among the feature-caching methods we compare; on Med-DDPM it also offers a better quality–speed trade-off than quantization, pruning, and fast-solver baselines. Our code is available at 

https://github.com/prinshul/Manifoldcache.git .

Why existing methods fall short on CMDMs. CMDMs employ fun-damentally different backbone ar-chitectures (volumetric convolutions, 

SE(3) -equivariant networks, graph transformers), whose feature dynam-ics are poorly matched by heuris-tics designed for pixel-space UNets and vision transformers. # 2 Related Work 

Constraint-manifold diffusion models. A growing class of diffusion models generates samples satisfying hard geometric, physical, or biological constraints [Dorjsembe et al., 2024, Jiao et al., 2024, Yao et al., 2025, Liu et al., 2026, Geffner et al., 2025, Frank et al., 2026, Kong et al., 2025]. Their backbones (volumetric 3D ConvNets, SE(3) -equivariant networks, graph transformers, and multi-view ViTs) are heavier than standard 2D UNets; constraint fidelity degrades sharply under step reduction since the probability flow is only tangent to Mc in the clean limit; and guidance strategies multiply NFEs by 2–4 ×. Together, these factors significantly increase the inference and generation times for CMDMs. 

Acceleration methods. Post-training quantization [Shang et al., 2023, Li et al., 2023, Huang et al., 2024, He et al., 2023, Feng et al., 2025, Gao et al., 2025, Wang et al., 2025] reduces per-step compute via INT8/FP8/INT4 weight and activation compression. On volumetric CMDMs, calibration passes exhaust GPU memory, resulting in Out of Memory (OOM) (mainly block reconstruction approaches), and quantization noise accumulates along directions normal to Mc, where any error directly manifests as constraint violation. Pruning. Magnitude [Han et al., 2015] and structural [Fang et al., 2023] pruning remove low-saliency weights to reduce per-step cost. On CMDMs every weight participates in satisfying the constraint map g; 30% sparsity already drives generation quality below an acceptable level, and neither variant reduces the NFE count. Fast solvers. DDIM [Song et al., 2020] and DPM-Solver++ [Lu et al., 2025] reduce NFEs by taking larger probability-flow ODE integration steps, exploiting trajectory smoothness in unconstrained image models. On CMDMs, large steps allow trajectories to drift off Mc before any corrective force restores them, producing constraint-violating samples. For discrete-diffusion variants such as DemoDiff [Liu et al., 2026], which operate on token simplices, high-order ODE solvers are categorically inapplicable. 

Feature caching. DeepCache [Ma et al., 2024], FasterDiffusion [Li et al., 2024], FORA [Selvaraju et al., 2024], SmoothCache [Liu et al., 2025c], TeaCache [Liu et al., 2025a], AB-Cache [Yu et al., 2025], PFC [Sommer et al., 2025], ToCa [Zou et al., 2024], dKV-Cache [Ma et al., 2026], Tay-lorSeer [Liu et al., 2025b], PAB [Zhao et al., 2025], AdaCache [Kahatapitiya et al., 2025], and FrDiff [So et al., 2024] exploit the near-stationarity of hidden activations between adjacent denoising steps via fixed intervals, calibration thresholds, input-difference proxies, or numerical extrapolation. These methods share a common limitation: none uses the geometric structure of Mc as a caching criterion, and none identifies ¯αt = 12 as a safe-caching boundary, so they may reuse features in the high-noise phase where, as Theorem 1 shows for a two-mode model, the modes are not yet resolved and reuse leads to mode confusion with non-vanishing probability. M ANIFOLD CACHE is the first framework to derive a depth-adaptive caching schedule with provable error bounds from constraint geometry, requiring no training, no calibration data, and no per-step runtime measurement. 

Speculative sampling. Speculative methods for diffusion models [De Bortoli et al., 2025] and diffusion language models [Agrawal et al., 2025] draft future denoising states with a cheaper proposal and then accept or reject them in a verification step, which reduces the number of expensive model calls while preserving the output distribution. M ANIFOLD CACHE works on a different axis: it keeps every denoising step and lowers the cost of each step by reusing block outputs according to a fixed schedule, with no draft model or verifier. The two approaches are therefore complementary; Appendix E discusses the differences in more detail and compares them on Proteina. 

# 3 Methodology 

MANIFOLD CACHE rests on a single observation: the physics or biology that defines a constraint manifold also determines when and where a denoiser’s computation can be safely skipped. We formalise this in four steps: (i) defining the CMDM class, (ii) decomposing the score geometrically, 

(iii) deriving a universal safe-caching threshold, and (iv) assembling a closed-form depth-adaptive schedule. Figure 2 illustrates the main ideas. 3Figure 2: Geometric picture behind M ANIFOLD CACHE . (a) Near Mc, the score splits into a normal part s⊥

3.1 Constraint-Manifold Diffusion Models 

Let X ⊆ RD be the ambient space , a voxel grid, sequence of residue coordinates, periodic unit cell, or latent video tensor depending on the application. Structured generative models do not place mass freely across X ; they confine samples to a surface carved out by domain knowledge. 

Definition 1 (CMDM) . A Constraint-Manifold Diffusion Model is specified by a C2 constraint map 

g : X × C → Rk and a conditioning signal c ∈ C . The target distribution p(· | c) is supported on the constraint manifold Mc := {x ∈ X : g(x; c) = 0 }. We assume ∇xg(x; c) has full rank k at every x ∈ M c (submersion condition), so by the Implicit Function Theorem Mc is a closed C2

submanifold of X of dimension dc := D − k.

State-of-the-art CMDM models across various domains are special cases: Med-DDPM [Dorjsembe et al., 2024] ( X = R192 3

, g enforces anatomical intensity and continuity), DiffCSP++ [Jiao et al., 2024] ( g encodes crystallographic space-group symmetry), Proteina [Geffner et al., 2025] ( X = R3N ,the Cα coordinates of N residues; g encodes Ramachandran validity and steric clash avoidance), SV4D 2.0 [Yao et al., 2025] and Lyra [Bahmani et al., 2026] ( g imposes multi-view photometric consistency). Despite radically different ambient spaces, all share three properties our theory exploits: 

g is externally specified, not learned (P1) ; codimension k ≫ 1 so a random point in X violates the constraints with probability approaching one (P2) ; and c is exact at every diffusion step (P3) .The constraint map g describes where the data lie: it is a modelling assumption about the support of p(· | c), not an operation performed at inference. M ANIFOLD CACHE never evaluates g, projects onto Mc, or checks constraint satisfaction; it reads only the timestep and the block index. How a model keeps its samples on Mc (through training data, conditioning, or an architecture that builds the constraint in) therefore does not affect how the schedule is applied. Table 9 in Appendix B lists the constraint and this mechanism for each of the eight models.

# 6 Conclusion 

We introduced M ANIFOLD CACHE , the first training-free acceleration framework designed specifically for Constraint-Manifold Diffusion Models (CMDMs). By exploiting the orthogonal decomposition of the diffusion score into manifold-normal and manifold-tangential components, M ANIFOLD CACHE 

derives a principled safe-caching threshold directly from the noise schedule together with a depth-adaptive caching policy grounded in score geometry. The resulting method requires no retraining, no calibration data, and negligible runtime overhead. Across eight diverse CMDMs spanning medical imaging, molecular generation, protein design, crystal prediction, and multi-view 4D synthesis, MANIFOLD CACHE consistently achieves superior quality–speed trade-offs and often simultaneously improves both inference time and generation fidelity over strong existing accelerators. Our results suggest that constraint geometry is not only central to generation quality, but also a fundamental signal for determining when and where diffusion computation can be safely reused. 10 References

23 A.6 Discussion 

Theorem 1 is proved under a two-mode Gaussian mixture (A3), the simplest setting in which the mode-flip probability can be computed in closed form. We use it as a simplified model: the data distributions of the evaluated models are multimodal and non-Gaussian, and we do not claim that they satisfy (A3). Theorem 1 is related to analyses of critical noise levels at which the modes of a mixture separate during sampling [Raya and Ambrogioni, 2023, Biroli et al., 2024, Li and Chen, 2024], and more broadly to theoretical studies of diffusion models on simple mixture distributions [Shah et al., 2023, Chidambaram et al., 2024]. Two parts of the analysis do not depend on (A3): the MMSE bound of Lemma 1 holds for any prior with covariance Σc, and Theorem 2 does not use (A3). Theorem 1 also does not use the smoothness or the reach of Mc; only Theorem 2 needs the tubular neighbourhood of (A2), and the runtime schedule never evaluates g. Codimension. The safe-side bound scales with tr Σ c, the total variance along the dc = D − k tangent directions, so for a fixed variance per direction a higher codimension k gives a smaller bound. Speculative methods and M ANIFOLD CACHE reduce cost in different ways. Speculative methods reduce the number of expensive model calls: a cheap proposal drafts future states, and the target 25 Figure 5: From left to right: M ANIFOLD CACHE , PAB, and AdaCache. It can be seen that M ANIFOLD CACHE

model then accepts or rejects them, which keeps the samples exactly distributed as those of the target model [De Bortoli et al., 2025, Agrawal et al., 2025]. M ANIFOLD CACHE has no draft model, verifier, or rejection step. It keeps every denoising step and lowers the cost of each one by reusing block outputs according to a fixed schedule that depends only on the noise level and the block depth. This leads to three practical differences. First, speculative methods need either a separate draft model, which is costly to obtain for 3D MRI or protein generation, or additional machinery. The training-free drafts of De Bortoli et al. [2025] require a stochastic sampler (the authors note that their method does not apply directly to deterministic samplers), and Spiffy [Agrawal et al., 2025] calibrates its draft graphs offline for discrete diffusion language models. De Bortoli et al. [2025] also note that, unlike large language models, diffusion models are not necessarily memory-bound, so parallel verification may bring smaller wall-clock gains. M ANIFOLD CACHE needs no calibration and runs on all eight backbones. Second, the guarantees differ: speculative sampling preserves the output distribution of the target model, whereas M ANIFOLD CACHE bounds the error of reuse and the probability of landing in the wrong mode (Theorem 1). Third, the two act on different parts of the trajectory: the analysis of De Bortoli et al. [2025] indicates that the acceptance rate is high at the beginning of denoising, which is the high-noise phase in which Theorem 1 advises against caching. In principle the two could therefore be combined, with speculation reducing the number of steps and caching reducing the cost of each remaining step; we leave this to future work. # G Limitations and Future Work 

Despite strong empirical and theoretical performance, M ANIFOLD CACHE has several important limitations that motivate future work. First, our theory relies on smooth closed manifold assumptions with well-defined tangent and normal decompositions. While these assumptions cover a broad range of scientific diffusion models, some structured generation problems involve non-smooth, discrete, combinatorial, or dynamically changing constraint sets where the local geometric decomposition may 26 not exist or may vary discontinuously across the trajectory. Extending manifold-aware caching to hybrid discrete–continuous diffusion processes, topology-changing manifolds, or non-Euclidean latent spaces remains an open direction . Second, the theoretical guarantees are derived under simpli-fied regularity and mixture assumptions designed to make the analysis tractable. Real CMDMs exhibit highly multimodal, non-Gaussian, and hierarchical distributions whose geometry may evolve across denoising stages. Although the ablations support the choice of T ∗, deriving sharper guarantees under more realistic score models, heavy-tailed distributions, or learned manifold geometries would strengthen the theoretical foundations of geometry-aware acceleration . In addition, the depth ordering of Theorem 2 relies on the regime condition κt < κ ∗, which we assume rather than derive. Third, the current schedule is analytically fixed from the noise schedule and block depth and does not adapt online to individual samples, prompts, or trajectory difficulty. Certain trajectories may approach the manifold substantially earlier or later than predicted by the global schedule, especially in highly multimodal settings. Future work could investigate sample-adaptive or uncertainty-aware caching policies that dynamically estimate manifold proximity, score curvature, or feature drift during inference while retaining the low-overhead nature of the current framework . Fourth, although M ANIFOLD CACHE is backbone-agnostic, the present implementation focuses on inference-time feature reuse and does not jointly optimize with other acceleration mechanisms such as learned schedulers, consistency distillation, speculative denoising, low-rank adaptation, or mixed-precision execution. Combining geometry-aware caching with solver acceleration, quantization, or token sparsification could potentially yield multiplicative speedups beyond those observed here . Fifth, our experiments focus on single-device inference on existing pretrained checkpoints. Large-scale distributed generation systems introduce additional communication and synchronization bottlenecks that may alter the trade-off between recomputation and cache reuse. Investigating geometry-aware caching under tensor parallelism, pipeline parallelism, or streaming generation settings is an important systems-level extension . Finally, our work studies geometry primarily as a mechanism for safe acceleration. A broader future direction is to treat manifold structure as a unifying principle connecting controllability, robustness, interpretability, and efficiency in scientific generative models . We believe CMDMs provide a promising foundation for studying these properties jointly, particularly in emerging multimodal settings combining physical simulation, language conditioning, molecular reasoning, and embodied 3D generation. 27
