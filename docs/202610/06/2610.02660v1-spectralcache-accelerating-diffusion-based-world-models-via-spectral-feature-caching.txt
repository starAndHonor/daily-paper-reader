## ABSTRACT 

Diffusion-based world models enable high-quality interactive environment genera-tion but suffer from substantial inference overhead due to repeated Transformer evaluations during denoising. Existing caching methods mainly exploit temporal redundancy at the feature or token level, leaving the underlying mathematical structure of diffusion features largely unexplored. In this work, we reveal that world-model features exhibit highly stable singular subspaces across nearby de-noising steps, while their singular values follow predictable evolution patterns. Building on this observation, we propose SpectralCache , a training-free spec-tral caching framework that reuses stable singular subspaces and estimates only low-dimensional singular values through linear extrapolation. We further exploit the spectral consistency between neighboring full-computation features to skip selected expensive backbone evaluations via singular value scaling. Extensive experiments on representative world models demonstrate that SpectralCache con-sistently improves inference efficiency while preserving generation quality. On HunyuanWorld-Voyager-13B, SpectralCache achieves 5.22 × acceleration while maintaining a WorldScore of 65.90 for static scenes, substantially outperforming existing training-free caching methods in inference efficiency.

Diffusion-based world models enable controllable visual simulation by predicting future observations conditioned on scenes and actions Bar et al. (2025); Zhu et al. (2025). These capabilities support applications ranging from visual planning to explorable 3D scene generation Bar et al. (2025); Huang et al. (2025). However, their iterative generation process requires repeated evaluations of large diffusion transformers (DiTs), incurring substantial computational cost and limiting responsiveness in

interactive applications Song et al. (2025); Feng et al. (2026b). Accelerating world-model inference while preserving generation quality is therefore a key challenge for practical deployment. A wide range of diffusion acceleration methods have been proposed to improve efficiency Feng et al. (2025b); Xi et al. (2025); Feng et al. (2025a); Zhang et al. Among them, caching methods based on token structural characteristics have attracted increasing attention for diffusion acceleration Chen et al. (2026); Selvaraju et al. (2024), due to their training-free nature and ability to preserve superior generation quality by reusing or predicting intermediate representations. However, most existing caching methods target conventional image and video diffusion models. They do not readily generalize to diffusion-based world models and direct adaptation often results in substantial quality degradation Song et al. (2025). Although recent studies have explored accelerating world model generation with caching methods Song et al. (2025); Feng et al. (2026b), they mainly focus on identifying world models’ differences from conventional diffusion models and exploiting how step-wise intermediate feature representations change for acceleration. Different from these works, in this paper, we explore the evolvement of underlying mathematical structure and parameter-level characteristics throughout generation, by using singular value decomposition (SVD). We perform SVD on the intermediate DiT features throughout world model generation and analyze how their underlying subspaces and singular values evolve across generation steps. Our insightful analysis reveals that: (1) Singular subspaces remain highly stable with similar singular vectors across consecutive steps. (2) Singular values of neighboring steps exhibit linear property, which enables accurate estimation of singular values through linear extrapolation or scaling. Building on these insights, we propose SpectralCache , a training-free caching framework for diffusion-based world models that efficiently accelerates video scene generation while preserving high generation quality. Specifically, across consecutive denoising steps, we approximate subsequent features by interpolating singular values within the singular subspace of the preceding fully computed feature, thereby skipping redundant full computations. When a full computation step is required due to large enough accumulation error Feng et al. (2026b), instead of always using full computations, we alternatively adopt full computation and approximation of singular values through scaling, yielding additional acceleration. Our comprehensive evaluation demonstrates that SpectralCache achieves superior speedup with non-marginal improvements than SOTA world-model acceleration methods Feng et al. (2026b); Song et al. (2025). Meanwhile, it leads to best generation quality compared with baselines due to highly accurate approximation. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose SpectralCache , a training-free spectral feature caching framework for acceler-ating diffusion-based world models. SpectralCache reuses stable singular subspaces and estimates only low rank singular values through linear extrapolation for cache and scaling for skipping more full computation, substantially reducing expensive overhead while preserving generation quality. • We reveal several interesting spectral structures in world-model features: they exhibit strong low-rank structure, while their dominant singular subspaces remain highly stable across both consecutive denoising steps and nearby cache-triggered full computation steps. Moreover, their singular values exhibit predictable linear extrapolation and approximately constant scaling patterns in these two settings, respectively, providing a new spectral perspective for understanding and exploiting temporal redundancy in diffusion-based world models. • Across two representative world models, HunyuanWorld-Voyager-13B and Aether-5B, SpectralCache consistently outperforms existing training-free caching methods in both generation quality and inference efficiency. On HunyuanWorld-Voyager-13B, SpectralCache achieves a 5.22 × speedup while maintaining WorldScores of 65.90 and 46.13 for static and dynamic scenes, respectively, compared with 3.65 × speedup and WorldScores of 64.89 and 45.43 achieved by WorldCache. 

## 2 RELATED WORKS 

Generation-driven Diffusion World Models. World models seek to learn predictive representations of the environment that support future-state anticipation, planning, and decision-making Hafner 2SpectralCache: Accelerating Diffusion-Based World Models via Spectral Feature Caching 

et al. (2019; 2023). Policy-centric world models Hafner et al. (2019; 2023); Robine et al. (2023); Wu et al. (2023) acquire environmental dynamics through agent-environment interaction, where action-conditioned observations and feedback are progressively incorporated into an internal model for subsequent planning and control. In contrast, generative world models Agarwal et al. (2025); Bar et al. (2025); Russell et al. (2025); Zhu et al. (2025) learn environmental dynamics from large-scale visual or video data and simulate plausible future scene evolution with diffusion method. 

Model-level Diffusion Acceleration. Despite their strong generative capabilities Yang et al. (2023); Croitoru et al. (2023), diffusion models still incur substantial inference costs, motivating various acceleration strategies. Optimization-based methods primarily modify the sampling process without altering the underlying denoising network, using deterministic sampling or advanced high-order solvers to reduce the number or cost of denoising steps Guo et al. (2024); Lu et al. (2022); Song et al. (2020). Recent studies further optimize ODE trajectories through distillation Luo et al. (2024); Yan et al. (2024); Yin et al. (2024), enabling generation with substantially fewer sampling steps, but often requiring additional optimization or training Salimans & Ho (2022). In contrast, model-structural acceleration methods directly reduces the computational complexity of the denoising network itself, such as compressing model parameters through quantization Shang et al. (2023); Wu et al. (2025) or removing redundant structures through pruning Zhang et al. (2024); Guo et al. (2025). 

Diffusion Acceleration from Representation Cache. Caching has recently emerged as an effective training-free approach for accelerating diffusion inference while largely preserving generation quality Kahatapitiya et al. (2025); Ma et al. (2024). Adaptive token caching selectively reuses tokens based on their importance or variation while recomputing the remaining tokens Zou et al. (2024); Zheng et al. (2025). Forecasting-based methods instead predict the evolution of intermediate representations to skip subsequent full computations across denoising steps Liu et al. (2025b); Chen et al. (2026). However, most existing caching methods are designed for conventional image or video generation and often suffer from quality degradation when directly applied to world models with complex hierarchical feature dynamics Song et al. (2025).

## 4 METHOD 

Based on our key observations and deep insights for the singular vectors and values of denoising features across different steps, we propose a spectral feature caching framework to accelerate diffusion-based world models. The core idea is to approximate diffusion features by reusing cached results of preceding full computation steps, thus skipping computations and achieving acceleration. Specifically, we define two kinds of diffusion steps: full computation step and approximation step. In full computation step, we go through the normal full diffusion computations and cache the singular vectors/values. In the approximation step, we do not perform the diffusion computations. Instead, the approximated features reuse the highly consistent singular subspaces from preceding full computation steps, while the singular values are estimated through linear extrapolation or scaling, following their respective spectral evolution patterns based on our observations and insights. 4.1 FULL COMPUTATION STEP 

At a full computation step t, we perform a complete DiT backbone evaluation to obtain the final layer visual feature Ft and reshape it into Xt = mat( Ft) ∈ RM ×D . Then we compute its rank-r spectral decomposition for potential subsequent cache steps: 

Xt = Urt Σrt (Vrt )⊤ + Rt, (4) where Urt , Σrt , and Vrt denote the top-r spectral subspaces and singular values, and Rt is the truncated residual. Based on our deep insights, we choose r = 128 since it captures 96% feature energy. We store the resulting spectral state Qt = {Urt , Σrt , Vrt , Rt} for subsequent steps. The spectral states of only two most recent full computation steps are cached for reuse. 4.2 APPROXIMATION STEPS 

At an approximation step, we bypass the full DiT backbone computation and reconstruct the visual feature by reusing the saved singular subspaces and residual while estimating only the dominant singular values. Specifically, the current visual feature at step t is approximated as 

bXt = Urn bΣrt (Vrn)⊤ + Rn. (5) where Urn and Vrn are spectral subspaces of the most recent full computation step. Since the leading singular subspaces remain highly stable across nearby denoising steps as demonstrated in Section 3, we directly reuse Urn and Vrn. The residual Rn is also directly reused since it accounts for only about 3% of the total feature energy on average throughout generation. 

bΣrt is estimated from singular values of previous full computation steps. We employ two estimation strategies according to the current caching state: linear extrapolation for consecutive cache steps and 5SpectralCache: Accelerating Diffusion-Based World Models via Spectral Feature Caching 

singular value scaling when a full computation step is triggered but can be safely skipped. Thus, each approximation step requires updating only r singular values while reusing other spectral components from the most recent full computation step, avoiding expensive DiT backbone computations. 4.2.1 SINGULAR VALUE CACHE THROUGH LINEAR EXTRAPOLATION 

We can estimate singular values through linear extrapolation for consecutive denoising steps. Specifi-cally, let m < n < t denote denoising steps, where m and n are the two most recent full computation steps and t is the current step to be approximated. Based on approximately linear evolution of singular values observed in Section 3, we can extrapolate singular values of current step t from step m and n:

bΣrt = Σrn + βt (Σrn − Σrm) , βt = t − nn − m , (6) As shown above, bΣrt is mainly determined by the step distance of m, n, t , and the difference of cached visual features Σrn and Σrm. Our analysis shows that this approximation empirically introduces only a 0.71% error relative to the corresponding ground truth fully computed features throughout denoising, showing that this approximation is highly accurate with minor errors. 4.2.2 SINGULAR VALUE ESTIMATION THROUGH SCALING 

We propose another way to estimate singular values. As observed in Section 3, the singular value mag-nitudes differ primarily by a constant scaling factor between nearby cache-triggered full computation steps. Thus, we can estimate singular values at full computaion step t as 

bΣrt = γΣrn, (7) where γ is a fixed scaling factor controlling the magnitude adjustment, and n < t denotes its most recent full computation step. We use γ = 1 .1 (measured in Section 3) for all main experiments. 4.3 APPROXIMATION ERROR ACCUMULATION TO TRIGGER FULL COMPUTATION 

For each diffusion step, we need to determine whether it is a full computation step or an approximation step. To complete this, we adopt an approximation error accumulation strategy to trigger full computation steps. In detail, although our efficient approximation steps introduces minor errors in each step, consecutive approximations across multiple denoising steps may gradually accumulate the estimation errors. When this accumulation error is large enough, we stop approximation and a full computation step is triggered to ensure high quality generation. Following Feng et al. (2026b), we monitor approximation error over chaotic tokens to determine when cached feature approximation should be terminated. ## 5 EXPERIMENTS 

5.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS 

Models. We evaluate our method on two SOTA mainstream diffusion based world models: HunyuanVoyager-13B Huang et al. (2025) and Aether-5B Zhu et al. (2025). Both models take images, text, and camera trajectories as inputs and jointly generate RGB videos and corresponding depth maps. These models take an initial image, an instruction, and camera poses as conditioning inputs to generate 3D videos comprising both RGB frames and scene depth. 

Datasets and Evaluation Metric. For the video generation datasets and evaluation metrics, we follow the experimental settings of prior works Song et al. (2025); Feng et al. (2026b). For generation quality evaluation, we similarly adopt WorldScore Duan et al. (2025), a comprehensive benchmark that assesses world generation in terms of both controllability and visual quality. Additionally, we evaluate the perceptual consistency of the generated videos by measuring their discrepancy from the no-cache baseline using PSNR, SSIM Wang et al. (2004), and LPIPS Zhang et al. (2018). Following the evaluation protocol of existing work Song et al. (2025), we also evaluate the 3D reconstruction metrics of generation videos with Sintel dataset Butler et al. (2012), which contains AbsRel, δ1, δ 2,ATE, and RPE-t/RPE-r. We also report speedup and peak memory. 

Baselines. We compare our method against several effective training-free diffusion model caching methods, including DuCa Zou et al., ToCa Zou et al. (2024), TaylorSeer Liu et al. (2025b), Hi-Cache Feng et al. (2026a), TeaCache Liu et al. (2025a), EasyCache Zhou et al. (2025), HERO Song et al. (2025), and WorldCache Feng et al. (2026b). 

Implementation Details. All experiments are conducted on two NVIDIA Pro 6000 96GB GPUs. We use a rank of 128 for all experiments and set γ = 1 .1. For other settings, we follow prior works Feng et al. (2026b); Zhu et al. (2025); Song et al. (2025) to make a fair comparison (see Appendix A.3). ## 6 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present SpectralCache , a training-free spectral feature caching framework for accelerating diffusion-based world models. Unlike existing caching methods that primarily exploit temporal redundancy at the feature or token level, SpectralCache leverages the underlying spectral structure of diffusion features. We reveal that world-model features exhibit stable singular subspaces across nearby denoising steps, while their singular values follow predictable evolution patterns. 9SpectralCache: Accelerating Diffusion-Based World Models via Spectral Feature Caching 

Based on these observations, SpectralCache reuses stable singular subspaces and efficiently estimates singular values through linear extrapolation for consecutive cache steps and singular value scaling for cache-triggered full computation steps, substantially reducing expensive backbone evaluations. Extensive experiments on representative world models demonstrate that SpectralCache consistently improves both generation quality and inference efficiency over existing training-free caching methods, achieving up to 5.22 × acceleration while preserving high generation quality.

Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete inference pipeline of SpectralCache. Given the initial latent, diffusion backbone, output head, and scheduler, we first perform two full backbone computations to initialize the spectral buffer B. For each full computation feature, we apply low-rank decomposition and store its singular subspaces, singular values, and residual, while retaining only the two most recent spectral states. These states provide the spectral information required for subsequent feature approximation without repeatedly evaluating the expensive diffusion backbone. After initialization, SpectralCache dynamically switches among spectral extrapolation, singular-value scaling, and true full computation according to the accumulated approximation error and cache interval. When the accumulated error remains below the threshold τ and the number of consecutive cache steps 

k is smaller than the maximum interval N , the current feature is approximated through spectral extrapolation. Specifically, we reuse the stable singular subspaces from the latest full computation state and linearly extrapolate its singular values from the two most recent full computation states. The corresponding residual is then added to reconstruct the current feature. After each approximation, the error of chaotic tokens is evaluated and accumulated to determine whether spectral extrapolation can continue. Once the accumulated error exceeds τ or the cache interval reaches N , a refresh is triggered to prevent excessive approximation error. Rather than directly performing an expensive backbone evaluation at every triggered step, SpectralCache further exploits the spectral consistency between neighboring full computation features. If the preceding refresh is a true full computation, the current triggered step is approximated by reusing its singular subspaces and residual while scaling the 15 SpectralCache: Accelerating Diffusion-Based World Models via Spectral Feature Caching 

singular values with factor γ, thereby skipping another expensive backbone evaluation. Otherwise, a true full computation is performed, and its newly decomposed spectral state is inserted into B to refresh the extrapolation basis. The approximation error and cache counter are then reset. Finally, the resulting feature is passed through the output head and diffusion scheduler to obtain the latent for the next denoising step.
