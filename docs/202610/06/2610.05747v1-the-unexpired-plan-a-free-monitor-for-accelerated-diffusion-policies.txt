# The Unexpired Plan: A Free Monitor for Accelerated Diffusion Policies 

# Yi Zhao Sebastian Scherer 

yizhao5@andrew.cmu.edu basti@andrew.cmu.edu 

AirLab, The Robotics Institute Carnegie Mellon University, Pittsburgh, PA, USA 

Project website: yizhaojasper.com/unexpired-plan/ 

Abstract — Training-free acceleration of a diffusion policy is accepted when an internal similarity signal reports that the shortcut changed nothing. We price every monitor a control loop can afford in closed-loop success rather than feature distance, over 114 accelerator configurations and four policy families: two accelerators each clear their own gate’s bar and log every reuse as certified, while on one task one finishes every episode and the other none. A monitor is two designs, not one — the statistic it reads, and what it does when that statistic fires. Published gates re-arm after every rejection, and under that response even an oracle handed every forward pass and the exact local action error loses twenty points; absorbing the same statistic costs one point, and most of its speed. What makes a response that never forgets affordable is a statistic that rarely fires, and what it must measure is deviation from the policy the accelerator replaced — which a chunked policy has already paid for, its last plan not yet expired and free to read. Guarding every call this way cuts per-call compute by 1.55 –3.09 ×, where any reference-requiring check at the same coverage would have to stop accelerating altogether. On three of our four families the schedule alone already holds the pre-stated ±2-point margin. What the monitor is measurably worth shows in three places: on the fourth family, where it rescues the candidate selection landed on; on four configurations it did not select; and on a contact-rich fifth family, chosen where the schedule was expected to fail and run after every design choice was frozen, where no unmonitored arm at its speed holds the margin and the monitored one does. unaccelerated benign harmful what the gate sees

I. Introduction Diffusion policies spend most of their inference budget recomputing what changed little since the previous control call; training-free accelerators exploit that by caching features, skipping denoising steps or reusing action chunks. Knowing when a shortcut is admissible is the unsolved half, and the field’s answer is to let the accelerator watch itself: compare a cached quantity against a freshly computed one and reuse while they agree. Closed-loop success is reported for the setting that ships, but the rule deciding each reuse is argued in feature space, a weak predictor of closed loop [1]. Validated in closed loop instead, the outcome is not a matter of degree: a cross-call cache in our sweep drives LIBERO-10 success from 97% to 6% (Fig. 1) while certifying every one of the 161 ,000 reuses it logs across all its episodes. Rather than report one failure we measured the whole design space. Section III prices every monitor a control loop can afford, over 114 configurations from four policy families, and the map is strongly negative: at a matched acceptance rate an exogenous gate does not beat discarding forwards at random, and an oracle handed every forward pass and the exact deviation still loses twenty points. What failed there is the response , not the signal, and the gating literature has been benchmarking signals. On the signal axis every free monitor Table I prices fails too: the one statistic that separates catastrophic from benign configurations is the deviation from the policy the accelerator replaced, exactly the quantity a free monitor cannot hold. It can be held, if the loop is read carefully. A chunked policy predicts 𝐻 actions and executes only 𝐻 exec < 𝐻 of them, so a reference evaluation leaves a plan still valid for the next 

⌊𝐻 /𝐻 exec ⌋ − 1 calls. Comparing the accelerator against that unexpired plan costs no forward pass and carries real reference provenance; at the one call in 𝑚 with no plan left we run the reference and execute it.

Related Work 

Training-free acceleration. Feature caching, step skipping and attention reuse accelerate video diffusion transformers per layer or per step [2], [3], [4], [5]. On robot policies, accelerators cache visual tokens [6], retrieve and refine chunks [7], carry a cache across inference chunks [8], re-emit the previous chunk when consecutive chunks agree [9], or exit early over depth [10]. These are the population we measure, not rivals we out-cache. Distillation reaches one step but retrains the weights [11], and a learned cache schedule trains a router [12]; both are outside our setting. 

Runtime monitoring. The closest work shows that gate provenance governs the closed-loop reliability of training-free skipping [13]; its actuation-slack refresh spends a forward to repair the skip, where we execute that forward and harvest its plan. Action-JND learns token tolerances from a frozen policy [14]; ours needs no learned estimator. Denoising-variance prefixes [15] and observation-conditioned caches [16] are the family we sweep in §III. The overlap of successive plans is itself well used: temporal ensembling averages what successive chunks predict for one index [17], bidirectional decoding scores their agreement to choose a sample [18], STAC [19] turns it into a runtime failure alarm, and chunked controllers read it to time a replan [9]. We neither average nor sample from it; we compare against it and veto, and what is new is what that costs: STAC draws fresh samples at both calls, where the unexpired plan is a reference evaluation already paid for. Table II prices the difference. Speculative decoding [20] verifies each draft token 

exactly against the target it replaces; ours is neither exact nor contemporaneous, which is why §V-A prices per-call misses (Eq. (4)). 

Offline search. Rollout-success search costs a GPU-day per task [21]; we score deviations, never success ( §IV-D). Real-time chunking alters chunk timing [22], outside our fixed schedule. III. Measuring what a monitor is worth 

Protocol. Every configuration is rolled out in closed loop whatever its monitor predicted, paired against the unaccelerated policy on identical initial states: 114 configurations over a video– action diffusion policy on LIBERO [23], [24], 𝜋 0.5 on LIBERO-10 [25], a Diffusion Policy on PushT [26], and 3D Diffusion Policy on MetaWorld [27], [28]. Monitors are instrumented on every evaluation, not only when they accept. Two responses are crossed throughout: re-arming restores permission after each rejection, absorbing does not. Rows of Table I that share a base 

guard the same accelerator on the same family, so the coin, the observation gate and the oracle are matched to each other and the last block guards the first row’s cache in place of its own gate; every comparison across rows below is within one such group. Under absorbing a veto retires the accelerator, or on the ladder row a rung; the last block’s accelerated share is capped at 

(𝑚 − 1)/ 𝑚 by its 𝑚 -periodic reference; and a coin matched to a gate’s retirement is absorbing by construction, hence two empty cells. 

A gate cannot be read from its own log. A gate records a value only when it accepts, so its maximum is bounded by its own threshold: the cross-call cache’s gate certifies every reuse it logs and, once instrumented on every call, accepts 53 .8% of them. 

The observation gate carries no information. Its safe settings accelerate too few calls to matter, every setting worth deploying certifies a collapse, and at a matched rate it does not beat a coin; an exogenous signal that cannot beat a coin is not a monitor. Unskipped computation does not see it either: the policy’s own value head ranks collapse no better than chance, inside the band reported for reactive detectors [13], and a finiteness check catches one collapse in 31. 

Granting and withdrawing are not symmetric. An admis-sion gate grants permission to reuse; a veto-only check withdraws it but never grants it. A false veto gives up acceleration, a false admission risks the task, and a fooled veto leaves the call exactly where an unmonitored loop would have left it — unsafe, but no less safe. §IV therefore grants nothing. 

Neither axis suffices alone, and the oracle shows why. 

Given every full forward and the exact local action error, thresholding still loses twenty points, and the natural reading, that local information is insufficient, is wrong: that oracle is an 

admission gate that re-arms after every rejection, so it keeps admitting chunks and small errors accumulate. Hold the statistic fixed, make the response absorbing, and the same oracle costs one point. That swing is real and it is not free. A rule that never forgets retires at its first rejection, so what absorbing costs is set by when that rejection lands — a property of how a statistic’s rejections cluster , not of its rate. The rate-matched coin rejects independently, so it admits under one call in expectation and the episode runs 𝜋 : safety under absorbing is never by itself evidence that a statistic carries information. The oracle’s rejections come in runs, so it still accelerates 2 .0% of the episode against 15 .3% re-arming, where an independent rule at its rate would not keep even one accelerated call in expectation — and that is what its one point costs. The observation gate at its safe settings, admitting early and rarely after, is why the column is not monotone in the accelerated share. The same clustering is why the control is a coin matched to the gate’s retirement rather than to its rate; on it the gate at its useful settings is indistinguishable from the coin. What decides whether stopping is affordable is the statistic, which is the argument for the last block: its check vetoes at most 1 .0% of the calls it examines on the arms we ship, and far fewer on three of the four, so absorbing costs it almost nothing. The two axes multiply, and the gating literature has varied only the first.

D. Selection and recovery 

We score a candidate by the 95th percentile of Eq. (1) over its calls — the tail, since an accelerator exact on most calls and badly wrong on a few has a small mean — and deploy the cheapest whose score clears 𝜃 . Scoring on a stream recorded from the unaccelerated policy is biased, a stateful accelerator replayed on reference observations never feeding its own error back in, so we roll each candidate out in closed loop under its own actions and score it against the reference evaluated on the states it visits, iterating until the score stops moving: deviations only, never rollout success, from the candidate’s own state distribution. Candidates are ordered by cost, so a veto steps to the next more conservative rung with both checks still active; the demotion is never undone within the episode, and only exhausting the ladder returns the policy to 𝜋 , on 1 .8% of episodes here.

Limitations Free coverage is a property of 𝑚 : a policy that executes its whole plan gets none, the speedup is capped at the reference period, and the method pays best at large 𝑚 . A per-call compute ratio buys neither episode time (the period is fixed) nor peak forward cost ( 𝑇 ≥ 𝐶 𝑓 pins it at the reference): it may save energy or increase policy-serving capacity, but we measure neither. We deploy on the expectation because nothing we measure separates independent misses from a sustained excursion: treat them as Poisson runs of 20 calls at 𝑝 𝑑 =1, 0 .36 runs an episode on 𝜋 0.5,and the deployed tail of the count rises to 0 .05. Eq. (4) prices 

per-call misses, so a bias too small to reach 𝜃 on any call adds nothing to FNR, and a rung is retired only by a veto; such a bias is bounded by candidate scoring, not by any per-call rule. 

𝑝 𝑑 is calibrated on random damage while what escapes FNR is selected for being undetectable, and every accelerated call is priced at one 𝑝 𝑑 while the uncovered call is the stalest of its period. 𝜃 is read off the deviation percentiles of the 114 configurations of §III; the arms of §V come from the same four families but not from that sweep, so the threshold is fitted to the families, not the arms, and distribution-free risk control [30] would need more than one held-out family. All closed-loop evidence here is in simulation, not on the real-robot distributions these policies serve [31]. VII. Conclusion Priced in closed-loop success rather than feature distance, the monitors a diffusion policy is offered buy less than their logs report: an accelerator’s gate certifies its own product, an exogenous gate does not beat random reuse, and exact local error at full compute still loses twenty points under a re-arming rule. What works is a comparison against the replaced policy, under a response that does not forget — and a chunked policy is already holding one: its unexpired plan. Harvesting it costs no forward pass and caps speed at the reference period. The core monitor implementation and project materials are available through the linked project website. References
