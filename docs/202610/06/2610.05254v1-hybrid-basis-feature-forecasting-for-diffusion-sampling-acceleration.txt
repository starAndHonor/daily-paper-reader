# Hybrid-Basis Feature Forecasting for Diffusion Sampling Acceleration 

Kai-Liang Cheng 1, Yuan-Yuan Cheng 1, Yu-fan Jin 1, and Xiao-Ming Fu 1 

> 1University of Science and Technology of China, China

Abstract We propose Hybrid-Basis Feature Forecasting (HybridFF ), a training-free, plug-and-play framework for accelerating diffusion sampling. To capture local smoothness, long-range trends, and complex non-monotonic variations when modeling feature evolution, HybridFF first estimates coefficients using moving least squares (MLS) for each of multiple complementary basis families and then combines the corresponding predictors using fusion weights. In addition to the choice of basis functions, the fusion weights also play a critical role. We introduce two strategies to balance quality and speedup. HybridFF (Fixed) prioritizes efficiency with model-specific fusion weights calibrated on a small set and held constant during inference. HybridFF (Adaptive) updates the fusion weights online using branch reliability scores computed from an exponential moving average of full-step prediction errors, improving prediction fidelity and generation quality under aggressive caching while retaining substantial acceleration. Experiments across DiT-XL/2, FLUX.1-dev, SD3.5-Large, and HunyuanVideo demonstrate a favorable speedup–quality trade-off over representative single-basis forecasters and caching baselines. 

Keywords diffusion models, sampling acceleration, feature forecasting, moving least squares 

1 Introduction 

Diffusion models [1], particularly those leveraging the Dif-fusion Transformer (DiT) architecture [2], have redefined the state of the art in generative modeling across multiple modalities, including high-fidelity image synthesis [3] and video generation [4]. However, the iterative nature of the denoising process remains a formidable barrier to efficient deployment. Each sampling step involves redundant, com-putationally expensive evaluations of the denoising network via deep stacks of attention and MLP modules, resulting in prohibitive inference latency [5, 6]. Consequently, developing efficient sampling acceleration techniques has become an important topic. Current acceleration methods can be broadly categorized into sampling step reduction [7, 8] and per-step computa-tion optimization [9, 10]. Among these, feature caching has emerged as a compelling training-free, plug-and-play solution that exploits temporal redundancy between adjacent timesteps [5, 11]. This approach has evolved from static reusing methods like DeepCache [11] and TeaCache [5], which assume feature invariance across steps, to dynamic forecasting frameworks exemplified by TaylorSeer [12]. Consistent with prior observations [12], Fig. 2(a) shows that intermediate diffusion features, including transformer attention/MLP outputs, follow structured temporal trends over local cache intervals rather than changing arbitrarily across adjacent denoising steps. Motivated by these predictable structures, many dynamic forecasting methods have been proposed. TaylorSeer [12] uses polynomial bases, which often deviate around sharp transitions, while HiCache [13] and Spectrum [14] introduce Hermite and Chebyshev polynomial bases, respectively, to improve the stability of feature forecasting under complex or long-range temporal dynamics. Similarly, TC-Pad´ e [15] introduces rational functions to better capture asymptotic behaviors. Despite their success, these forecasting methods typically rely on a single basis family or a uniform temporal prior to model feature dynamics. In practice, diffusion features exhibit highly heterogeneous dynamics across different timesteps, noise levels, network layers, and individual samples [6, 15, 16]. A single basis inevitably imposes a rigid inductive bias that cannot adapt to the complex, non-monotonic variations inherent in the de-noising process. This one-basis-fits-all dilemma restricts the generation quality at aggressive acceleration ratios, leading to larger feature-prediction errors than hybrid-basis forecasting (Fig. 2(b)). To overcome this limitation, we propose Hybrid-Basis Fea-ture Forecasting (HybridFF) , a novel training-free frame-work that models diffusion feature evolution as a weighted composition of multiple complementary basis families. The core insight of HybridFF is that different mathematical bases possess unique strengths, e.g., Taylor expansions [12] are effective for modeling local smoothness, Hermite polynomi-als [13] provide stable interpolation with derivative-aware constraints, and Chebyshev polynomials [14] offer robust approximation over broader intervals. 2 Related Works 

Diffusion Models and Generative Applications. Diffusion models have become a dominant paradigm for high-quality generative modeling across image synthesis, conditional gen-eration, and video generation [1–3, 17–21]. Among them, diffusion transformers (DiTs) significantly improve scalability and generation quality by replacing convolutional backbones with transformer architectures [2]. These advances have en-abled diffusion-based systems to be applied to a broad range of tasks, including photorealistic text-to-image generation [3, 18–20], video generation [21–23], and restoration tasks such as super-resolution and inpainting [24, 25]. However, despite their strong generation capability, these models remain computationally expensive at inference time due to the iter-ative denoising process, which motivates efficient diffusion inference methods. 

Diffusion Sampling Acceleration. Existing methods for ac-celerating diffusion inference mainly follow two directions. The first reduces the number of denoising steps through Hybrid-Basis Feature Forecasting for Diffusion Sampling Acceleration 3improved numerical solvers, consistency-style learning, or distillation-based trajectory compression. Representative ex-amples include DDIM [26], DPM-Solver [7], consistency models [8], and progressive distillation [27]. The second re-duces the computational cost of each denoising step through model simplification, structural pruning, quantization, token reduction, or architectural acceleration [9, 10, 28–31]. Among these approaches, training-free methods that avoid retraining the generative model are particularly attractive, since they can better preserve the original model behavior and are often applicable in a plug-and-play manner. 

Feature Caching and Feature Forecasting. Feature caching has recently emerged as an effective training-free paradigm for diffusion acceleration. Early methods mainly follow a cache-then-reuse strategy, where intermediate activations computed at previous timesteps are directly reused to skip redundant computation in later steps [5, 11, 32–34]. While exploiting temporal feature similarity enables low-overhead speedups, direct reuse becomes unreliable at large skip intervals, lead-ing to error accumulation and quality degradation. Recent works therefore shift to cache-then-forecast , predicting future features from historical activations rather than copying them. TaylorSeer [12] frames forecasting as feature extrapolation, establishing it as a stronger alternative to naive reuse. Subse-quent works improve forecasting via Adams-Bashforth-style extrapolation [35], plug-in predictor upgrades [36], spectral or global trajectory modeling [14], and trajectory-consistent rational approximation [15]. Other extensions further incorpo-rate adaptive forecasting mechanisms [16, 37–40]. Together, they show that forecasting-based acceleration offers better speed–quality trade-offs than direct reuse. 

Single-Basis and Hybrid-Basis Forecasting. Despite di-verse formulations, most forecasting methods still rely on a single prior or basis family, ranging from local extrapolation and global/spectral fitting to adaptive, speculative, subspace-aware, or learnable predictor designs [6, 12–16, 36–39, 41]. While effective in their target regimes, a single predictor form imposes a restrictive bias: local predictors may struggle with long-range evolution, whereas global ones may miss local trend changes. Since diffusion features exhibit diverse tempo-ral patterns across timesteps, layers, and samples, relying on a single basis family can limit prediction flexibility under ag-gressive skipping ratios. In contrast, we explore hybrid-basis forecasting , which combines multiple complementary basis families. HybridFF therefore captures local behavior, long-range trends, and complex non-monotonic feature evolution during diffusion sampling.

3.4 Fixed and Adaptive Fusion Weights 

We report two variants under the same coefficient-fusion pipeline. They differ only in how the simplex weight πti is determined: HybridFF (Fixed) calibrates π once offline and freezes it, whereas HybridFF (Adaptive) updates π online from full-step prediction errors. 

Fixed weights. The fixed weight is calibrated once by a lightweight simplex grid search over a small candidate set, scoring each candidate by SSIM against the full-step reference on a small calibration subset (a few percent of the prompts), and then frozen for all inference steps and prompts. This is a one-time hyperparameter calibration. Because the weight is a single dimension-free simplex vector shared across steps, Hy-bridFF (Fixed) adds essentially no runtime overhead beyond the coefficient mix itself. Per-backbone calibration details are given in Sec. 4.5. 

Adaptive weights. A single fixed weight cannot track feature dynamics that vary across samples, layers, and timesteps. This subsection expands on the scheduling and complexity discussion. 

Schedule. HybridFF separates the scheduler from the pre-dictor. We support two policies: (i) fixed-interval scheduling with an initial full-step warm-up and an optional full-step tail, and (ii) flex-window scheduling in which the inter-full-step interval grows monotonically through sampling, mirroring the budget allocation pattern observed in [14]. Both policies feed an ordered Iti to the predictor; no part of the predictor depends on the schedule type.

4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

Models and protocols. We evaluate HybridFF on class-conditional image generation with DiT-XL/2 [2], text-to-image generation with FLUX.1-dev [20] and SD3.5-Large [42], and text-to-video generation with Hunyuan-Video. We use the standard ImageNet protocol for DiT-XL/2, DrawBench prompts for FLUX.1-dev and SD3.5-Large, and VBench prompts for HunyuanVideo. The 50-step sampler serves as the quality anchor on every benchmark, and all experiments are conducted on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPUs. 

Baselines and hyperparameters. We compare against three representative forecasting baselines: TaylorSeer [12] (Tay-lor extrapolation), HiCache [13] (Hermite forecasting), and 8 K.-L. Cheng, Y.-Y. Cheng, Y.-F. Jin, and X.-M. Fu Spectrum [14] (Chebyshev forecasting). Unless otherwise specified, all forecasting baselines use order O = 4 or degree 

D = 4 , and HybridFF additionally sets the history window to K = 5 .

Metrics. We report both efficiency metrics (wall-clock la-tency and speedup w.r.t. the 50-step reference) and quality metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS w.r.t. the 50-step output; FID, IS, sFID, Recall, and Precision for class-conditional gener-ation; CLIP-Score and ImageReward for text-to-image; and VBench quality for text-to-video). 

Note on latency. HybridFF is consistently slightly faster than Spectrum across all benchmarks even though it evaluates three branches. Spectrum performs its Chebyshev fit over the 

entire cached history, whereas HybridFF restricts every MLS branch to a short window ( K = 5 in all experiments) and uses a closed-form Taylor branch with no matrix solve. The cubic 

O(K3) MLS solve is therefore much smaller, and this gain outweighs the cost of the extra branches and the simplex mix.

5 Conclusion 

We presented HybridFF , a training-free, plug-and-play fea-ture forecaster that accelerates diffusion sampling by com-bining complementary temporal bases within a unified MLS framework. The method is motivated by the empirical obser-vation that intermediate transformer features follow structured trajectories along denoising steps, making skipped-step fea-tures predictable from cached full-step observations. Fusion happens in coefficient space on the simplex with calibrated weights, followed by an anchored residual reconstruction; the predictor is linear, dimension-free, much cheaper than a full block evaluation, and provably no worse than the best single branch. We also design two weighting strategies: a Fixed variant calibrated offline for speed, and an Adaptive variant updated online from full-step errors for fidelity. Across DiT, FLUX.1-dev, SD3.5-Large, and Hunyuan-Video, HybridFF dominates single-basis baselines on every primary fidelity metric, and the optimal mixture varies sub-stantially across backbones, confirming that no fixed temporal prior is universally best. HybridFF (Fixed) emphasizes la-tency reduction, while HybridFF (Adaptive) improves fidelity by updating fusion weights from full-step feature prediction errors without extra denoiser evaluations. The current study leaves broader basis libraries, schedule-aware full-step/predict-step policies, and finer reliability es-timation underexplored.
